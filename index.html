The story

After her brother Noah dies, Christine Carpenter refuses to accept it. At his funeral she locks everyone inside the church and forces Michael, the funeral home’s hearse driver, to hit the gas — bound for Stull, Kansas, one of the seven supposed gates to Hell.

The ritual works. Sort of. Noah’s body comes back, but a demon named Purson is driving it. And Christine forgot one thing: Noah was an organ donor. Now Purson has his hooks in every recipient.

Armed with the thank-you cards their families sent, Christine tracks them down one by one — Through this night of Hell, Christine learns how far she’ll go to put her family back together, piece by piece.

Piece by piece.

The passengers

Christine

The sister

Scared of blood, terrible under pressure, rarely thinks more than one step ahead. No secret badass underneath — just someone who refuses to give up on her family.

Michael

The driver

Overlooked his whole life. Sees Christine’s impossible mission as a chance to finally matter — until it’s hard to tell if he’s helping her or exploiting her.

Leon

The boyfriend

Small-town mailman, lovable himbo, Noah’s best friend first. A beer cooler, a compound bow, and no idea what he’s gotten into.

Purson

The passenger

20th of the 72 demons. Keeper of secrets, leader of hordes — and newly the owner of a human body. Locked in the back, yet always one step ahead.

Tonal comps

Sometimes self-aware, always scary.

The filmmakers

Jacob & Ben Burghart — the Burghart Brothers — are Kansas-based writer-directors blending suspense, dark humor and inventive visual storytelling. Their feature debut, HEAD COUNT, starring Ryan Kwanten, was released theatrically by Shout! Studios and is streaming on Starz and Netflix Australia.

Josh Doke directed GOODLAND (acquired by Showtime), co-founded Mutiny Pictures, and executive produced SKINAMARINK — named Variety’s and Rolling Stone’s best horror film of 2023.
